# M5 provider source decision

**Research checked:** 2026-09-25. This is a technical source assessment, not legal advice, provider approval, a purchase decision, or permission to make a live request.

## Decision

The production posture remains **BLOCKED**. The current CoinGecko and Etherscan fixture integrations prove parser and replay mechanics only. No candidate source has demonstrated every M5 capability with the exact scope, temporal, pagination, snapshot and audit guarantees required by the existing domain contracts. Storage, authority persistence, redistribution and commercial use also lack explicit provider-specific approval.

The machine-readable file [`config/m5/provider-readiness.production.json`](../config/m5/provider-readiness.production.json) is scoped only to CoinGecko's `coingecko-market-chart` dataset/version. It records the three market-series capabilities exposed by that dataset and all seven usages as `REQUIRES_APPROVAL`. It does not represent the other providers or the combined production stack. The existing `m5-provider-readiness-config/v1` contract binds one provider/dataset/version; the separate `m5-provider-readiness-aggregate/v1` composes authentic, scope-matched results without changing that contract.

The authoritative single-scope usage enum contains exactly seven values: `NETWORK_ACQUISITION`, `RAW_PAYLOAD_PROCESSING`, `RAW_PAYLOAD_STORAGE`, `NORMALIZED_STORAGE`, `AUTHORITY_PERSISTENCE`, `REDISTRIBUTION`, and `COMMERCIAL_USE`. Aggregate policy carries those exact seven values independently for each provider; provider access remains `NETWORK_ACQUISITION`, and raw storage remains `RAW_PAYLOAD_STORAGE`. It also records a separate `RETENTION` decision. Retention is a data-lifecycle constraint (how long material may be kept and when it must be deleted), not an additional value in the existing provider usage enum. It is checked independently and cannot authorize raw storage, normalized storage or any other usage. Thus the aggregate has seven inherited usage decisions plus one separate retention decision per source; it does not redefine the seven-value source contract.

## What M5 needs

M5 consumes evidence through versioned normalized source packages and sealed `SourceLineage`. Downstream authorities require exact material membership and fingerprints. The provider layer must therefore identify what was observed, when it was available, which asset/dataset/version it belongs to, and whether the declared universe is actually complete. A successful HTTP response or a large sample is not a completeness proof.

For every provider response, request/retrieval receipt time is distinct from observation time. M5 must retain both. Availability is never inferred from a provider's historical timestamp alone. Holder and venue completeness additionally need an explicit enumeration boundary and a reconciliation result.

## Official source findings

### CoinGecko

Relevant products/endpoints are CoinGecko API historical chart range by ID or token address (`/coins/{id}/market_chart/range` and `/coins/{id}/contract/{contract_address}/market_chart/range`) for price, market cap and total volume. The endpoint accepts a requested date range; automatic granularity is five-minute for current-day data, hourly for shorter historical ranges, and daily at 00:00 UTC above 90 days. The last completed UTC daily point is published at 00:35 UTC. Basic is limited to the past two years; Analyst and above provide longer history subject to source availability. The arrays do not by themselves establish a complete M5 asset set or the domain's exact close semantics. Published pricing currently lists Demo $0 (10k credits/month, 100 requests/minute, attribution); Basic $35 month-to-month ($29/month equivalent billed yearly), 100k credits and 300/minute; Analyst $129 month-to-month ($103.20/month equivalent billed yearly), 500k credits and 500/minute. Check current plan details before any decision.

GeckoTerminal's Top Pools endpoint returns up to 20 ranked pools per page; pages beyond 10 require Analyst or above. Its documentation describes liquidity/volume ranking, not an exhaustive pool universe. CoinGecko's paid API plan table describes commercial licenses and API terms require prominent “Powered by CoinGecko” attribution. The terms discourage caching/storage; if data is stored, the terms describe refresh and deletion/security conditions, and restrict copying, storing or deriving data except where expressly permitted. The standard license also restricts API/data redistribution and points custom redistribution/white-label needs to Enterprise or case-by-case review. This does not settle retention or derived-record rights for this product; review the selected plan and terms before use.

Official sources: [API documentation](https://docs.coingecko.com/), [Top Pools endpoint](https://docs.coingecko.com/reference/top-pools-contract-address), [pricing/plans](https://www.coingecko.com/en/api/pricing), [API terms](https://www.coingecko.com/en/api_terms).

### Etherscan

Relevant endpoints include `getsourcecode`, `getcontractcreation`, token holder list (`tokenholderlist`), top holders (`topholders`), historical total supply (`tokensupplyhistory`), historical account balance and ERC-20 transfer logs. The holder-list endpoint is current-state and paginated (`page`, `offset`); the separate top-holders endpoint is explicitly capped at 1,000. Historical supply is block-number-addressable and marked PRO. The published plan page currently lists Free at $0/3 calls per second/100k daily calls, Lite at $49 monthly, and Standard at $199 monthly with 10 calls per second, 200k daily calls and PRO endpoints. Token holder list and top holders are PRO and the top holders endpoint is throttled to 2 calls per second.

Etherscan's holder-list docs expose current-state rows with `page` and `offset` but document no block parameter, maximum page size, atomic snapshot across pages, block-hash binding or finality proof. The separate `topholders` endpoint caps `offset` at 1,000 and is throttled to two calls/second. Combining the current holder list with block-addressable historical supply does not meet M5's same-block/hash snapshot contract. Etherscan's API terms describe API content as personal/informational and restrict commercial use, resale and redistribution absent express prior written consent; Premium terms separately say a paid tier grants no commercial or redistribution rights. The API terms require written approval for external sharing and retention beyond the otherwise permitted period; the reviewed terms do not give this decision a retention period to rely on. API terms state last updated 2026-06-15. Verify current terms before any future decision.

Official sources: [API endpoint overview](https://docs.etherscan.io/endpoint-overview), [contract source](https://docs.etherscan.io/api-reference/endpoint/getsourcecode), [holder list](https://docs.etherscan.io/api-reference/endpoint/tokenholderlist), [top holders](https://docs.etherscan.io/api-reference/endpoint/topholders), [historical supply](https://docs.etherscan.io/api-reference/endpoint/tokensupplyhistory), [plan/rate pricing](https://etherscan.io/apis), [API terms](https://etherscan.io/apiterms), [Premium terms](https://etherscan.io/premium-terms).

### Alchemy

Alchemy is a candidate RPC/archive transport, not an M5 market-data or complete holder provider. Its current `eth_getLogs` plan table lists Ethereum range width 10 for Free and unlimited for PAYG/Enterprise, with every response capped at 150 MB. The endpoint returns matching logs and includes block number/hash in log objects; this is not a guarantee of holder completeness, finality, or a provider-created atomic holder snapshot. An independently operated indexer could reconstruct balances from history and reconcile supply, but token-specific behaviors and exhaustive processing remain future engineering work, not an implemented authority.

Published pricing lists 30M compute units/month in Free, then $0.525 per million CUs on PAYG; method costs and throughput add-ons vary, and no monthly workload was specified, so a monthly price cannot be responsibly estimated. Alchemy's general terms grant service use for internal business purposes and restrict resale/sublicensing of the service; the public terms reviewed do not resolve the exact customer-facing use, retention or redistribution rights for M5 RPC-derived records. Those require product/order-form review and approval. [Pricing](https://www.alchemy.com/pricing), [eth_getLogs](https://www.alchemy.com/docs/chains/ethereum/ethereum-api-endpoints/eth-get-logs), [compute-unit pricing](https://www.alchemy.com/docs/reference/compute-unit-costs), [legal terms](https://legal.alchemy.com/).

### Bitquery

Bitquery's EVM `Holders` API documents top-holder queries, counts, thresholds and limit/order pagination; it explicitly says the Holders cube does not support `Block.Date`. The separate Balances API documents point-in-time address balances by date, but that alone is not a complete token-holder universe tied to one exact block hash/finality proof. Current pricing lists Personal $49 monthly / $39 monthly equivalent billed yearly (personal-use, non-commercial) and Pro $99 monthly / $79 monthly equivalent billed yearly (commercial-use license). Historical data is an add-on; Transfers/Balances/Holders are listed at $150/month per chain ($120/month equivalent billed yearly for named EVM chains). Pricing advertises historical holder snapshots for every wallet, but reviewed public docs do not establish M5 block-hash/finality semantics or an auditable page termination contract. Enterprise pricing is custom and advertises complete historical data/bulk delivery; no product-specific offer was obtained.

Official sources: [holder API](https://docs.bitquery.io/docs/schema/evm/token-holders/), [balances API](https://docs.bitquery.io/docs/blockchain/Ethereum/balances/), [pricing/history/licensing](https://bitquery.io/pricing), [terms](https://bitquery.io/terms-of-service). The reviewed terms page did not establish M5-specific output retention or redistribution rights; keep those usage decisions `REQUIRES_APPROVAL`.

### GoPlus Security and DEX Screener (narrow candidates)

GoPlus documents Token Security and Address Security API products for selected token/address risk fields. Its support page says the Security API is free with a 30 calls/minute limit; higher limits require contacting GoPlus for an access token. These signals do not establish every M5 suspicious rule, a rule-by-rule evaluation over our exact SourceLineage, or holder-snapshot block/finality binding. Product-specific retention, redistribution and commercial-use rights were not established in reviewed official materials; record these as `UNKNOWN` pending review, not as a proved permission or prohibition.

DEX Screener documents token-pair lookup (`/token-pairs/v1/{chainId}/{tokenAddress}`), pair lookup and search at 300 requests/minute. These return pair-level records useful for discovery, but the API reference provides no exact total-count/end-of-universe proof or protocol-registry guarantee. They cannot establish full liquidity or venue sets by themselves. Its API terms state commercial and non-commercial use is allowed subject to listed limits, while prohibiting making the API or any portion available to third parties. Storage and use of derived M5 material need product-specific review; the terms page was last updated 2023-08-18, so recheck it before a decision.

Official sources: [GoPlus Token Security](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/reference/tokensecurityusingget_1), [GoPlus Address Security](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/reference/addresscontractusingget_1), [GoPlus rate limits](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/reference/support), [GoPlus API overview](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/reference/api-overview), [DEX Screener API reference](https://docs.dexscreener.com/api/reference), [DEX Screener API terms](https://docs.dexscreener.com/api/api-terms-and-conditions).

## Recommended provider stack

1. **Minimum technical candidate stack:** CoinGecko for market series and market-cap/volume candidates; Etherscan for contract source/verification and explorer metadata; GoPlus as a partial security-signal source; Alchemy or an equivalent Ethereum archive RPC for independently replaying block-pinned transfer logs; plus an internally operated, versioned indexer and reviewed contract/protocol registries. This is a research proposal only. It still does not solve complete cross-DEX venue enumeration, all suspicious inputs/rule coverage or canonical identity by itself.
2. **Lowest priced public-tier combination for a limited technical evaluation:** CoinGecko Demo ($0, attribution and low limits), Etherscan Free ($0, 3 calls/sec, selected chains/community endpoints) and Alchemy Free (30M CU/month). This does not meet M5 completeness, retention or production-use approvals.
3. **Indicative monthly list-price combination for technical planning:** CoinGecko Basic $35 + Etherscan Standard $199 = **$234/month**, plus Alchemy PAYG usage as applicable ($0.525 per million CUs; first 30M CUs/month are included in Free tier terms). Analyst in place of Basic gives **$328/month** at monthly list prices, but only permits deeper ranked-pool paging. CoinGecko annual billing lowers the displayed monthly equivalent ($29 Basic / $103.20 Analyst); Etherscan annual/quarterly offers may also change the effective rate. These are arithmetic combinations of public list prices, not quotes, approved budgets, or permission for a commercial deployment. Etherscan's published terms require express written consent for commercial use; CoinGecko commercial rights depend on selected paid plan and redistribution is separately restricted.
4. **More robust candidate:** Bitquery Pro plus Ethereum Transfers/Balances/Holders archive is currently listed at $99 + $150 = **$249/month** month-to-month, or $79 + $120 = **$199/month equivalent** with displayed annual billing for those products, before workload/add-ons. Bitquery Enterprise has custom pricing and advertises broader complete-history/bulk products. Pair either with independently verified archive RPC and future deterministic holder/indexer work. Neither public plan-price nor Enterprise marketing establishes M5 block-hash/finality semantics or legal approval.

All cost figures are public list-price snapshots checked 2026-09-25. They exclude taxes, workload-dependent CU/credit overages, data engineering, infrastructure, observability, legal advice and custom licenses. Annual-equivalent figures require annual billing. Enterprise/contact-sales prices are custom/unknown. No amount is a vendor quote, approved budget or binding total; prices/rate limits can change and do not prove completeness or authorize purchase/use.

## Decisions Robert must make

- Define supported chains and a canonical asset onboarding/universe process.
- Set required historical depth and accepted gap policy for daily series.
