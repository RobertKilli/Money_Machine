# M5 provider source decision

**Research checked:** 2026-09-25. This is a technical source assessment, not legal advice, provider approval, a purchase decision, or permission to make a live request.

## Decision

The production posture remains **BLOCKED**. The current CoinGecko and Etherscan fixture integrations prove parser and replay mechanics only. No candidate source has demonstrated every M5 capability with the exact scope, temporal, pagination, snapshot and audit guarantees required by the existing domain contracts. Storage, authority persistence, redistribution and commercial use also lack explicit provider-specific approval.

The machine-readable roll-up is [`config/m5/provider-readiness.production.json`](../config/m5/provider-readiness.production.json). It uses the existing `m5-provider-readiness-config/v1` contract and is evaluated by the existing readiness evaluator. It contains no `APPROVED` usage decisions and must evaluate `BLOCKED` for the default complete M5 capability and usage request.

## What M5 needs

M5 consumes evidence through versioned normalized source packages and sealed `SourceLineage`. Downstream authorities require exact material membership and fingerprints. The provider layer must therefore identify what was observed, when it was available, which asset/dataset/version it belongs to, and whether the declared universe is actually complete. A successful HTTP response or a large sample is not a completeness proof.

| `SUSPICIOUS_REQUIRED_INPUTS` | Every input required by the pinned suspicious rule set: source/code/ownership/upgradeability/security signals and their explicit unknown states as defined by that rule set. | Inputs must be bound to the same candidate, mapping revision and source lineage; evidence times must not exceed as-of. Any chain-specific snapshot inputs need consistent block scope. | Multiple sources and freshness checks; persist input evidence and versioned rule set. | `m5-suspicious-rule-set/v1`, raw suspicious evidence and mapping/lineage bindings. |
| `SUSPICIOUS_RULE_COVERAGE` | An explicit evaluation for every required rule over the exact material set. No omitted, sampled or unsupported rules may become CLEAN. | Exact equality between required/evaluated rule IDs; all evidence available by as-of, with lineage IDs/fingerprints preserved. | The cost is per rule and input source; retain immutable coverage and assessment material. | `m5-suspicious-coverage-authority/v1` and `m5-suspicious-assessment/v1`; incomplete coverage cannot produce a clean result. |
| `CANONICAL_ASSET_IDENTITY` | A reviewed mapping from chain namespace and contract address to the product's canonical asset identity. Provider IDs/symbols are supporting identifiers only. | Bind the assertion to provider/dataset/version, source artifact/envelope and payload fingerprint; mapping validity has its own recorded/valid time. | Low per-asset lookup volume, but requires human review and auditable correction history. | `m5-provider-asset-identity-assertion/v1` and mapping revision/SourceLineage contracts. |

For every provider response, request/retrieval receipt time is distinct from observation time. M5 must retain both. Availability is never inferred from a provider's historical timestamp alone. Holder and venue completeness additionally need an explicit enumeration boundary and a reconciliation result.

## Official source findings

### CoinGecko

Relevant products/endpoints are the CoinGecko API Pro historical chart endpoints (`/coins/{id}/market_chart/range` and the contract-address variant) for price, market cap and volume, plus GeckoTerminal on-chain DEX endpoints such as `GET /api/v3/onchain/networks/{network}/tokens/{token_address}/pools` for ranked pools. Historical chart access depends on plan and asset coverage. The published pricing page currently lists Demo at $0 with attribution, 10k credits/month and 100 requests/minute; Basic at $35 monthly ($29/month billed yearly), 100k credits and 300/minute, with two years daily history; Analyst at $129 monthly ($103.20/month billed yearly), 500k credits and 500/minute, with ten years daily history. Check current plan details before any decision.

The Top Pools endpoint returns up to 20 pools per page; pages beyond 10 require Analyst or above. It is explicitly ranked by liquidity/volume and does not claim to return every pool. CoinGecko's pricing/terms describe standard commercial plans and attribution, but prohibit API/data redistribution absent a suitable separate/custom agreement. The page's “commercial” label is not approval to retain or redistribute Money Machine's normalized/persisted records. Exact use, retention and derived-data rights need a provider-specific review.

Official sources: [API documentation](https://docs.coingecko.com/), [Top Pools endpoint](https://docs.coingecko.com/reference/top-pools-contract-address), [pricing/plans](https://www.coingecko.com/en/api/pricing), [API terms](https://www.coingecko.com/en/api_terms).

### Etherscan

Relevant endpoints include `getsourcecode`, `getcontractcreation`, token holder list (`tokenholderlist`), top holders (`topholders`), historical total supply (`tokensupplyhistory`), historical account balance and ERC-20 transfer logs. The holder-list endpoint is current-state and paginated (`page`, `offset`); the separate top-holders endpoint is explicitly capped at 1,000. Historical supply is block-number-addressable and marked PRO. The published plan page currently lists Free at $0/3 calls per second/100k daily calls, Lite at $49 monthly, and Standard at $199 monthly with 10 calls per second, 200k daily calls and PRO endpoints. Token holder list and top holders are PRO and the top holders endpoint is throttled to 2 calls per second.

Etherscan's current holder-list docs do not describe an atomic block-pinned holder-list snapshot, nor bind all pages to a block hash or finality proof. Combining a current list with a historical supply response would not meet the M5 snapshot contract. Its terms restrict the licensed use to the subscribing user's applications; redistribution/external sharing and storage beyond permitted retention require express written approval. API/premium access also does not itself grant commercial or redistribution rights. The terms page was updated 2026-06-15 when checked; legal review must use the terms in force at the time of any future request.

Official sources: [API endpoint overview](https://docs.etherscan.io/endpoint-overview), [contract source](https://docs.etherscan.io/api-reference/endpoint/getsourcecode), [holder list](https://docs.etherscan.io/api-reference/endpoint/tokenholderlist), [top holders](https://docs.etherscan.io/api-reference/endpoint/topholders), [historical supply](https://docs.etherscan.io/api-reference/endpoint/tokensupplyhistory), [plan/rate pricing](https://etherscan.io/apis), [API terms](https://etherscan.io/apiterms), [Premium terms](https://etherscan.io/premium-terms).

### Alchemy

Alchemy is a candidate RPC/archive transport, not an M5 market-data or complete holder provider. `eth_getLogs` can return matching logs and supports block-range queries; documented maximum range for Ethereum is 10 blocks on Free and unlimited on Pay As You Go/Enterprise, with a 150 MB response cap. A client could use block-pinned RPC calls and independently reconstruct a holder state, but must cover all relevant history and token-specific behaviors and reconcile the result. This is an engineering/indexing project, not a completeness guarantee from the endpoint.

Published pricing lists 30M compute units/month free, then $0.525 per million CUs on PAYG; method costs vary, and no monthly workload was specified, so a monthly price cannot be responsibly estimated. [Pricing](https://www.alchemy.com/pricing), [eth_getLogs](https://www.alchemy.com/docs/chains/ethereum/ethereum-api-endpoints/eth-get-logs), [compute-unit pricing](https://www.alchemy.com/docs/reference/compute-unit-costs), [legal terms](https://legal.alchemy.com/).

### Bitquery

Bitquery's EVM `Holders` API provides ranked holder queries/counts, and its `Balances` API can query an address at a historical date/block. The official holder docs show top-holder queries with a limit and explain that `Holders` does not support `Block.Date`; historical holder filtering uses `Balance.LastChangeTime`. This is useful discovery/reconciliation evidence, but does not establish one complete holder set pinned to the M5 block number/hash. Bitquery documents archive history and bulk data products at Enterprise level; the public self-serve plans have rolling windows and separate archive add-ons. Pricing is currently Personal $39/month and Pro $79/month billed annually ($99 month-to-month), with Ethereum archive add-ons from $70/month; Personal is non-commercial. Enterprise pricing is custom. These prices do not prove that a suitable holder snapshot or license is included.

Official sources: [holder API](https://docs.bitquery.io/docs/blockchain/Ethereum/token-holders/token-holder-api/), [balances API](https://docs.bitquery.io/docs/blockchain/Ethereum/balances/), [pricing/history/licensing](https://bitquery.io/pricing), [terms](https://bitquery.io/terms-of-service).

### GoPlus Security and DEX Screener (narrow candidates)

GoPlus documents `GET /api/v1/token_security/{chain_id}` for token security/risk fields and `GET /api/v1/address_security/{address}` for address-risk data. Its support page currently says the Security API is free at 30 calls/minute; higher limits require contacting the provider for an access token. These point-in-time token/address checks can support selected suspicious inputs, but the documented API does not assert that it covers every M5 rule, provide a rule-by-rule assessment for our exact SourceLineage, or bind outputs to the holder snapshot's block/finality. The public materials reviewed did not establish the API's retention/redistribution/commercial terms for this product, so usage remains `REQUIRES_APPROVAL`.

DEX Screener documents token-pair lookup (`/token-pairs/v1/{chainId}/{tokenAddress}`) and pair/search endpoints, generally at 300 requests/minute. These return pair-level market/liquidity records useful for discovery, but the docs do not provide an exact total-count/end-of-universe proof or protocol-registry guarantee. They cannot establish the full liquidity or venue sets by themselves. DEX Screener's API terms permit commercial and non-commercial use subject to restrictions, and prohibit making the API/data available to third parties; applicability to stored/derived M5 material needs review.

Official sources: [GoPlus Token Security](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/reference/tokensecurityusingget_1), [GoPlus Address Security](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/reference/addresscontractusingget_1), [GoPlus rate limits](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/reference/support), [GoPlus API overview](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/reference/api-overview), [DEX Screener API reference](https://docs.dexscreener.com/api/reference), [DEX Screener API terms](https://docs.dexscreener.com/api/api-terms-and-conditions).

## Recommended provider stack

1. **Minimum technical candidate stack:** CoinGecko for market series and market-cap/volume candidates; Etherscan for contract source/verification and explorer metadata; GoPlus as a partial security-signal source; Alchemy or an equivalent Ethereum archive RPC for independently replaying block-pinned transfer logs; plus an internally operated, versioned indexer and reviewed contract/protocol registries. This is a research proposal only. It still does not solve complete cross-DEX venue enumeration, all suspicious inputs/rule coverage or canonical identity by itself.
2. **Lowest priced candidate for a limited, non-production evaluation:** CoinGecko Demo ($0, attribution and low limits), Etherscan Free ($0, 3 calls/sec, selected chains/community endpoints), and Alchemy Free (30M CU/month). This does not meet M5 completeness, source retention or production-use approval.
3. **Lowest priced published plan combination for a modest commercial research deployment:** CoinGecko Basic monthly $35 + Etherscan Standard monthly $199 = **$234/month**, with Alchemy's first 30M CUs/month free and additional PAYG CUs at $0.525/M. This is not a production-ready M5 stack: M5 holder completeness/finality, venues and suspicious coverage remain missing; CoinGecko redistribution and Etherscan storage/sharing terms need express review. If more than 10 pool pages are needed, CoinGecko Analyst currently raises the listed subtotal to **$328/month** (Analyst $129 + Etherscan Standard $199), still only ranked pools.
4. **More robust candidate:** quote Bitquery Enterprise for complete-history/bulk products alongside a separately verified Ethereum archive RPC and our own deterministic holder/indexer pipeline; retain CoinGecko/Etherscan only for their narrower data. Enterprise pricing is custom. No quote was requested and no complete-coverage or legal claim is made.

Monthly estimates exclude tax, workload-dependent CU/credit overages, data engineering, infrastructure, observability, legal advice and custom licenses. Prices/rate limits are point-in-time vendor listings, can change, and do not demonstrate completeness or authorize purchase/use.

## Decisions Robert must make

- Define supported chains and a canonical asset onboarding/universe process.
- Set required historical depth and accepted gap policy for daily series.
- Decide if normalized provider-derived records, source artifacts and authority material may be retained, for how long, and whether anything is shown/shared outside the private application.
- Decide commercial product model, user count, data attribution and redistribution needs before selecting plans.
- Approve provider-specific usage only after reviewing the current plan and terms; obtain written permission where the published terms require it.
- Decide whether to fund and operate a block-pinned indexer and protocol/venue registry, or pay for a vendor product whose contract explicitly guarantees the required completeness and snapshot semantics.
- Have qualified counsel review the applicable terms, derived-data/storage rights, privacy, jurisdiction and commercial model. This document makes no legal determination.

## Before the first live request

Every item below must be completed in a separately scoped, approved phase:

1. Provider and dataset versions, exact endpoints, chain/asset scope, rate limits and outage behavior are documented and reviewed.
2. For every required capability, documented product behavior and a testable reconciliation prove complete coverage, exact pagination termination, timestamps, and block/hash/finality bindings where applicable. Top-N/sample endpoints do not qualify.
3. M5 adapters exist for the selected provider data and pass strict fixture tests, malformed/unknown-field rejection, scope/version binding, replay determinism and lineage tests.
4. Robert records explicit, unexpired approval for `NETWORK_ACQUISITION`, `RAW_PAYLOAD_PROCESSING`, and every intended storage, authority persistence, redistribution and commercial usage. Written provider permissions are attached/referenced where required. Never put terms text or secrets in config.
5. An operator approves credentials, secret handling, budgets, request limits, monitoring, retention/deletion, incident handling and rollback. These are not performed in this slice.
6. The production readiness config is updated from reviewed evidence and evaluates `READY` for the exact provider/dataset/version, complete capability set and requested usages. Execution still requires the authenticated scope-matched gate.

Until then, no live request, credential, scheduler or production persistence path is authorized. Current posture is **BLOCKED** because capabilities remain partial/unknown and all usage approvals are `REQUIRES_APPROVAL`.
